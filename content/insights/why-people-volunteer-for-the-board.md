Nobody runs for the board for the free coffee.

They run because something they care about felt unprotected.

A roof that keeps getting deferred. A reserve study that sat unread. A neighbor who got steamrolled in a meeting. Or the quieter pull: “If I don’t do it, who will?”

Volunteers usually show up for one of a few reasons — and the reason shapes how they lead.

**Protect the asset.** Some join because the building is their largest investment. They want maintenance funded, vendors held to scope, and surprises turned into line items. Their risk is becoming the person who only talks dollars and forgets that owners live here.

**Protect fairness.** Others join after watching process get skipped — selective enforcement, closed-door deals, or a clique that always wins. Their risk is turning every issue into a fight about “the rules” instead of solving the underlying problem.

**Protect community.** A third group wants the place to feel like home: amenities that work, meetings that don’t feel hostile, a culture where people can disagree without becoming enemies. Their risk is prioritizing peace over hard numbers until the bill arrives.

**Protect the record.** A smaller set joins out of duty — retired officers, attorneys, engineers who know what “fiduciary” actually means. Their risk is drowning the board in process while the parking lot still floods.

None of these motives are wrong. The trouble starts when a board never names them.

A board that only has “protect the asset” voices underinvests in communication. A board that only has “community” voices underfunds the roof. Mix the motives on purpose. Ask every candidate — and every sitting director — a plain question: *What are you here to protect?*

Then write it down. When the lobby vs. roof argument shows up (it will), you already know whose lens you’re hearing.

Serving is unpaid. The accountability isn’t. Knowing why people volunteered is how you keep the work pointed at owners, not at whoever talks loudest.

If you’re on a board — or thinking about it — what’s the real reason you said yes?
